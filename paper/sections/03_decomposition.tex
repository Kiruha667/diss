Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} separates task difficulty, model identity, and trajectory information on the article population of \numsrc{e15.ne.runs@L66}{$7\,732$} submitted runs with non-empty patches. Task difficulty estimated from other models' outcomes alone reaches \numsrc{e15.ne.D@L68}{$0.926\,[0.912;\,0.936]$}; this is a ceiling, not a deployable predictor. Within a task, model identity alone discriminates better than the trajectory models, so pooled performance should not be read as evidence of attempt-level discrimination.

Beyond task difficulty and model, structure adds \numsrc{e15.diff.DMS_DM.ne@L110}{$+0.001\,[-0.000;\,+0.002]$} AUROC. The gain of $M+S$ over model identity plus length is \numsrc{e15.diff.MS_MLEN.ne@L108}{$+0.003\,[-0.003;\,+0.009]$}; over model identity plus the log step count it is \numsrc{e15.diff.MS_MN.ne@L107}{$+0.005\,[-0.003;\,+0.012]$}. Trajectory structure is not distinguishable from model identity plus length, or plus the number of steps alone.

The model remains useful for triage: ranking submitted runs by risk puts about twice the base rate of failures into the top tenth, with lift \numsrc{e15.lift.ne@L106}{$1.89$} against an unresolved proportion of \numsrc{e15.ne.base@L65}{$0.319$}. Its recall at that budget is \numsrc{e15.ne.MS.rec@L93}{$0.189$}, compared with a maximum possible \numsrc{e15.ne.maxrec@L96}{$0.313$}; the similar precision of $M+LEN$ in Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} limits what this application establishes about structure.
